thread·lands /ˈθrɛdˌlændz/ noun

  1. The land that ties a city to what keeps it alive: the rivers that water it, the farms that feed it and the wild places that cool and shelter it.
  2. A planning tool for seeing those ties and making them stronger.

Every city lives on water, farms and wild places.

Threadlands helps planners and communities plan a city together with the land that sustains it. See how heat, flooding, food and wildlife connect across a region. Then test your plan against the climate of 2050 and find where to act first.

    What each thread carries

    Water

    Flood or supply

    Rain that falls upstream reaches the city as a flood or as a steady supply. Wetlands, healthy soils and street trees decide which.

    Heat

    Stored or released

    Dense surfaces hold heat through the night. Shade, water and nearby vegetation pull it back out of the air.

    Food

    Buffer or exposure

    A region that grows more of its own food has more buffer when supply chains break. Farmland managed for soil is also habitat.

    Habitat

    Islands or network

    Species move along connected land. A city park and a forest stay separate islands until a corridor joins them.
