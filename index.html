thread·lands /ˈθrɛdˌlændz/ noun

  1. The land that ties a city to what keeps it alive: the rivers that water it, the farms that feed it and the wild places that cool and shelter it.
  2. A planning tool for seeing those ties and making them stronger.

Every city lives on water, farms and wild places.

Plan a city together with the land it depends on. See how heat, floods, food and wildlife connect, then test your plan against 2050. Free, for any place on Earth.

    What each thread carries

    Water

    Flood or supply

    Upstream rain arrives as a flood or a steady supply. Wetlands and trees decide which.

    Heat

    Stored or released

    Pavement holds heat all night. Shade, water and green pull it back out.

    Food

    Buffer or exposure

    Farms close by are a buffer when supply chains break.

    Habitat

    Islands or network

    A park and a forest stay islands until a corridor joins them.
