                  Rough costs and benefits

                  What this plan might cost and return across the whole map, city and surrounding land, at US prices. A first-pass estimate for comparing plans, not a budget.

                  Sources and assumptions
                  • Street trees: about US$1,400 to $2,700 to plant one street tree, including two years of contractor care, in New York City (2015 and 2019 to 2020, amNY). Yearly care US$13 to $65 and yearly benefits US$31 to $89 per tree, from shade, energy savings, cleaner air, stormwater and property value, across five US cities (McPherson and others, 2005). The street-tree lever at 100 adds about 850 trees per km² of built-up land, roughly New York City’s street-tree density.
                  • Wetlands: about US$19,000 to $23,000 per acre (US$47,000 to $57,000 per hectare) to restore, in US Army Corps districts, and over US$100,000 per acre in the largest coastal cities (2022 review of Environmental Law Institute survey data). Inland wetlands provide on average about US$25,700 per hectare per year in flood control, water supply, habitat and other services, with a very wide range between sites (de Groot and others, 2013). Salt marshes cut flood damage by about 18% on average in one New Jersey county (Narayan and others, 2017). Costs here assume the whole hex is restored, so they are an upper bound.
                  • Corridors: median land restoration costs from US$185 to $3,012 per hectare depending on method (One Earth, on a study in Land Degradation & Development). Restored forest provides about US$3,000 per hectare per year in temperate regions and US$5,300 in the tropics (de Groot and others, 2013).
                  • Regenerative farming: cover crops cost about US$37 per acre per year (US$90 per hectare) for seed and seeding, and often pay back in two to three years through better soil and lower inputs (USDA SARE).
                  • Compact growth is mostly a zoning decision rather than a construction cost. The most sprawling areas of Halifax, Canada, cost the city about three times as much per household each year as the most compact ones (Halifax Regional Municipality study, summarized by Streetsblog).
                  • Prices by country: about 70% of each cost and benefit is scaled by the country’s price level compared with the United States, for local labor and services, and about 30% stays at US prices, for materials and equipment traded worldwide. Price levels come from World Bank data: the PPP conversion factor divided by the official exchange rate. Taiwan and Cuba have no World Bank price data, so they are shown at US prices.
                  • Ecosystem values are averages across many studies and are not cash returns. Benefits and costs are not discounted over time.
